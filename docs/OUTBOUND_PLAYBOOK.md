## 1 Recommended strategy

### Build around buying requirements and verified cars

Your tech-sales experience transfers well to account research, contact mapping, follow-up and measurement. The strongest adaptation is to build a library of **standing buying requirements**: exact model, specification, budget basis, destination, purchase route and timing. When a suitable car becomes available, you already know which buyers to contact.

Use two routes together. For a verified available car, find businesses that already trade or operate that type. For a promising buyer, obtain a buying brief and match it against Optimum's authorised supply. A supplier list alone cannot tell you whether either side has a workable deal.

The 26 September audit covered 38 public adverts: 21 Mercedes-Benz, 11 BMW and six other vehicles. The concentration in G63, S-Class, BMW 7 Series and X5 suggests practical starting lanes. These are adverts, not proof of 38 unique, company-owned, immediately available VINs. The earlier inventory research remains the source for the individual advert details.

### Four buyer groups and what each needs

**Independent prestige dealers.** Best first prospecting lane for G63, high-spec SUVs, S-Class and specialist vehicles. Find the owner or used-car buyer. The proposition is a specific saleable unit at a workable trade cost, with credible documents and delivery. They need enough margin after preparation, funding, warranty exposure and selling costs. A public retail asking price is not their buying budget.

**Chauffeur operators with purchasing authority.** Best fit for suitable S-Class and 7 Series specifications. Find the owner, fleet director or operations director. Discuss rear-seat comfort, passenger capacity, luggage, annual mileage, downtime, operating approvals and replacement dates. Black paint and a famous badge are insufficient. Ask whether they own, lease or subcontract the cars and who actually purchases replacements.

**Specialist luxury rental operators.** Possible fit for G63, X7, Range Rover and selected performance cars. Find the owner or fleet buyer. The conversation concerns bookable demand, commercial insurance, seasonality, depreciation and disposal. An advertised daily rate does not reveal actual utilisation or profit. Ask which class earns its place in their fleet.

**Brokers and referral partners.** Concierge firms, hotel transport managers, security operators and leasing brokers can introduce demand. A hotel that advertises limousine transfers may outsource every journey. Qualify the operator behind the service. Agree company-approved referral terms and customer ownership before sharing introductions or promising fees.

Manufacturers themselves are generally poor first targets for reselling a small number of sourced luxury cars. Their authorised retailers, independent specialists and fleet customers are the useful account universe. Large rental groups can matter later, but a local branch manager may have no purchasing authority.

### Where to start across Europe

Use **Czechia and nearby EU markets** to learn the process with shorter travel and delivery routes. Add **Benelux** specialist dealers and chauffeur fleets; **Spain**, particularly Madrid and the Mediterranean luxury-rental markets; **Italy** for prestige dealers and NCC operators; and **France** for dealers and chauffeur businesses around Paris and the Riviera. This is a research sequence, not a ranking of proven profit opportunities.

A European address does not make the economics identical. For each destination, confirm registration charges, vehicle eligibility, warranty coverage, VAT treatment, language and transport. UK right-hand-drive demand reduces the natural fit for many left-hand-drive cars. Norway and Switzerland are outside the EU and require separate import analysis. A high local retail price can disappear into taxes, costs and dealer margin.

### A useful reason for someone to take the meeting

Offer a ten-to-fifteen-minute review with a defined result: determine whether a specific car fits, or record a buying brief that prevents irrelevant offers. If the buyer simply wants the dossier and can decide by email, support that route. A meeting is a tool for moving a real deal forward; do not force every experienced trade buyer into a SaaS-style demo.

Your differentiation can be accuracy, useful timing and follow-through. Automotive already has sophisticated digital trading: AutoProff describes a European B2B platform and its buyer guidance includes trading chat. Fleet Europe also runs a remarketing community. The defensible interview claim is that you can apply these disciplines particularly well for Optimum, rather than that the whole industry has yet to discover software. [AutoProff](https://www.autoproff.com/about-us) · [Fleet Europe Remarketing](https://fleeteuroperemarketing.com/en)

## 2 Public sources and permitted access

### The first sources I would build around

**Recommended starting combination:** OpenStreetMap for a geographic company seed list; ARES for Czech entity verification; France's business-search API for a second EU market; company websites for model fit and published contact details where automated access is permitted; and opt-in buyer requirements from your own conversations. Add a small licensed Kompass sample if contact discovery becomes the bottleneck. Use marketplaces manually or through expressly authorised access.

There is no verified single open database containing every European luxury dealer, its current buyer, public phone, email and live purchasing intent. Separate company discovery, commercial fit, contact research and channel permission. A directory can be excellent for one and weak for the others.

**Access labels used below:**

- **Documented API or open data:** a published machine-readable route exists. Follow its licence, allowed fields, limits and privacy conditions.
- **Commercial access:** request an approved API, export or feed. Pay for the rights your workflow actually needs.
- **Manual research or permission first:** the source is useful, but this research did not establish permission to automate extraction. A public page is not a scraping licence.
- **Member or platform use:** participate under the community's rules. Membership does not grant permission to copy the member database or send bulk unsolicited messages.

### 2A Company databases for a future tool

**OpenStreetMap and Overpass API — Europe and worldwide.** Start with mapped car sellers using the `shop=car` tag and rentals using `amenity=car_rental`. Records may include name, address, brand, website and telephone, but completeness varies and luxury status is not reliable. Use Overpass for bounded queries or a suitable data extract for larger work. Follow the ODbL licence, attribution and applicable share-alike obligations; do not overload public endpoints. This is a practical open-data foundation, followed by website qualification. **Access: documented API and open data.** [Overpass documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API) · [Car shop tag](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag%3Ashop%3Dcar) · [OSM licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)

**ARES — Czechia.** Use the Ministry of Finance's public API to match trading names to registered entities, IČO, address and available registry information. It is especially useful for deduplicating Prague businesses and checking the invoicing entity. The ministry publishes current API documentation and operating restrictions, including limits on requests, concurrent queries and random database probing. Use supported searches, not guessed identifier enumeration. Registry activity descriptions do not prove luxury specialisation or current buying. **Access: documented public API subject to operating conditions.** [ARES documentation and conditions](https://mf.gov.cz/cs/ministerstvo/informacni-systemy/ares)

**Annuaire des Entreprises and API Recherche d'entreprises — France.** An open business-search API combines core public French company data, including information from Sirene and the national business register. Use company identity, location, activity and available management fields to build or verify French accounts. Follow published diffusion and reuse restrictions. Do not confuse this with the separate restricted API Entreprise for authorised public-service uses. Registry records are not a complete email or buying-intent database. **Access: documented open API; Sirene bulk data is a separate route for larger projects.** [Official API explanation](https://annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr/donnees/api-entreprises) · [API documentation](https://recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr/docs/)

**Companies House — United Kingdom.** The public data API supports company and officer research; the officer endpoint requires an API key. Useful for identifying a legal entity and checking whether a named director is current. It does not establish who purchases cars or supply a dependable sales email/mobile number. Use business contact channels, not residential information in filings. Prioritise UK buyers only where vehicle specification and import economics fit. **Access: documented API with its current policies and limits.** [Developer hub](https://developer.company-information.service.gov.uk/) · [Company officers](https://developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk/companies-house-public-data-api/reference/officers/list)

**Brønnøysund Register Centre — Norway.** Provides organisation datasets and API documentation, including entity search and roles. The open-data material is offered under the Norwegian Licence for Open Government Data; some other datasets have different access requirements. Useful for legal identities, activity codes and available business contact fields, followed by website verification. Norway is a separate import market, so qualify the vehicle economics early. **Access: documented open-data APIs and downloads; use only the permitted public fields.** [Datasets and API](https://www.brreg.no/en/use-of-data-from-the-bronnoysund-register-centre/datasets-and-api/) · [Entity API documentation](https://data.brreg.no/enhetsregisteret/api/dokumentasjon/en/index.html)

**Kompass EasyBusiness — international.** A commercial prospecting database with company and executive filters, contact information and advertised Excel/PDF export. This is closer to the contact database you described than most car marketplaces. Ask for a sample of 25–50 European prestige dealers or chauffeur companies before committing. Measure correct company matches, current roles, working office numbers and coverage by country. Export permission is contract-specific and does not establish marketing consent. **Access: commercial export or separately agreed integration.** [EasyBusiness product and export features](https://www.solutions.kompass.com/our-products-services/sales-marketing-solutions/easybusiness/)

## 3 From company discovery to a qualified meeting

### Step 1 Create a verified supply record

Before offering a car, ask Optimum for authority to sell it and record the VIN or identifiable allocation, actual location, availability, model year, registration date, mileage, equipment, price basis, VAT regime, warranty and earliest realistic delivery. Check manufacturer build information against the advert. Record who confirmed each critical fact and when the offer expires. An advert URL alone is not sufficient evidence for “available now.”

### Step 2 Discover the company and remove duplicates

Use an approved source to identify a trading name, website, country, city and legal identifier where available. Match the website's legal notice against a registry. Keep a parent-company field so five branches do not become five supposedly independent buying decisions. Exclude repair-only businesses, inactive entities and irrelevant low-price stock. Treat a same-name match as unresolved until the address or legal identifier agrees.

### Step 3 Establish a reason this company might buy

Record one or two dated facts: it lists G63s; its chauffeur fleet includes S-Class; it advertises a car-buying service; it has announced a new location. Link the original page. Label fleet size and ownership as company-claimed or unknown. Listing several cars does not mean the business bought them, owns them, has sold them quickly or wants more.

### Step 4 Find the role before hunting the person

For an independent dealer, start with owner, managing director, vehicle buyer or head of used cars. For a chauffeur or rental company, start with owner, fleet manager or purchasing lead. In a large group, find the central procurement function. An operations director may specify a car while finance or a leasing company controls its purchase.

Find names from the company team page, legal notice, official register, recent company announcements and manual LinkedIn research. Corroborate name and current role where practical. If none is published, a business-switchboard question can be more useful than guessing: “Who handles buying vehicles for your own stock or fleet?” Do not infer personal email addresses or mobile numbers from a name.

### Step 5 Record business contact routes and their source

Prefer the company's own published office number, relevant purchasing/stock email or contact form for the purpose stated. Separate office switchboards, business mobiles and personal contacts; do not silently merge them. Record the exact source and date, language, time zone, named role and verification status. A purchased contact should retain its supplier and permitted-use record.

AI can classify businesses, extract permitted published fields and propose a model match. Require a source beside every name, phone and email. An invented executive or a guessed email is a failed record, not a partially completed lead. Validate extracted phone formats and domains, and let a human resolve uncertain identity matches.

### Step 6 Select a valid approach route

This matters for the system you described. **Data access and marketing permission are separate checks.** Public availability or an API licence does not automatically authorise email, telephone marketing, WhatsApp or promotional social messages.

The Czech ÚOOÚ states that publicly available electronic contact details cannot simply be used for advertising email; prior consent or the qualifying existing-customer exception is required. It also says an unsolicited email asking for marketing consent can itself be a commercial message. Do not interpret an info@ address, a bought database or an unsubscribe line as solving this. Use the email examples below after a valid request/permission, or where the applicable rules otherwise permit the contact. [ÚOOÚ commercial communications FAQ](https://uoou.gov.cz/index.php/profesional/qa-otazky-a-odpovedi/obchodni-sdeleni)

Phone rules depend on the country, source and circumstances. Have Optimum establish a country/channel rule before a calling campaign, including required do-not-call checks, permitted calling hours and how consent or another applicable basis is documented. A public business number is not a universal exemption. The Czech regulator publishes a specific telemarketing interpretation; check it alongside the rules for the destination country. Promotional LinkedIn or WhatsApp messages are not an automatic workaround. [ČTÚ telemarketing guidance](https://ctu.gov.cz/sites/default/files/obsah/ctu-new/telekomunikace/telemarketing/vykladove-stanovisko-telemarketing-a-faq.pdf)

Where cold outreach is unsuitable, use trade-platform enquiries within their rules, events, referrals where the recipient agrees to an introduction, relevant inbound content and an opt-in stock bulletin. For identified people, record the appropriate privacy basis, source information, notice and objections; restrict retention to what the business needs. [European Commission guidance on lawful processing](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en)

### Step 7 Earn a short meeting with one useful question

Make the reason for contact specific and ask a question that distinguishes a potential buyer from an attractive-looking company. “Do you buy G63s for stock or only against customer orders?” is useful. “Are you interested in luxury cars?” is not.

If there is fit, book 10–15 minutes with the actual buyer. State the agenda, two possible times and the buyer's time zone. Obtain the correct email and permission for the promised material; record the scope. Permission for one requested dossier does not automatically cover an indefinite newsletter.

### Step 8 Qualify and advance the opportunity

Capture the model and generation, essential options, acceptable substitutions, exterior/interior, seats, steering side, year/mileage, quantity, delivery destination/date and acceptable invoice basis. Ask whether they buy, lease or broker; who signs; how finance is arranged; and whether a trade-in or end-customer order is involved. Ask what would prevent purchase even if the specification fits.

Then compare the **actual delivered economics**: acquisition price on the correct VAT basis, transport, relevant non-recoverable taxes, registration/homologation, preparation, funding costs, warranty exposure and the buyer's required margin. Optimum's finance/logistics team must confirm the binding figures and terms.

Record one dated next step: build-sheet review, finance check, inspection, supplier confirmation or quote. A pleasant chat without a requirement and next action is a contact, not a qualified opportunity.

### A restrained contact sequence

For accounts with an approved outreach route, test one initial approach, the promised material if requested, and one useful follow-up at an agreed time. If there is no reply, allow one later attempt with a genuine new reason, then pause. This is a proposed pilot cadence, not a legal entitlement to make three attempts. Stop on objection. Use the buyer's stated replacement month to reopen a relevant conversation.

The valuable long-term asset is a small set of buyers who have told you exactly what to bring them. Offer an opt-in alert covering their models, budget and timing. Allow them to change or stop it easily. Prefer accurate individual matches over a daily broadcast of every advert.

## 4 Calls and messages

### Before using any example

These are original templates, not quoted scripts from a sales book. Use them only when you represent Optimum with its authority and have an approved contact route. While still a candidate, use them as interview demonstrations. Replace brackets with verified facts; never claim a held car, buyer, discount, owner relationship or delivery deadline you do not have. If supply is unconfirmed, use the buying-brief version.

Speak calmly. A professional buyer usually cares more about whether the car and numbers are real than whether you sound excited. Give the confirmed price when asked; do not hide it merely to force a meeting.

### Cold call 1 Independent prestige dealer

**Aim:** identify the buyer and book a brief trade-stock review for a verified G63.

**Opening:** “Hi [name], Max from Optimum Prague. I saw the [specific G63 listing] on your own site. Have I caught you for 30 seconds to explain why I'm calling?”

**Reason:** “We have authority to offer a [year, exact model] in [colour], with [one material option], at [confirmed price and VAT basis]. I thought it might fit the stock you already carry.”

**Qualification:** “Do you buy that model into stock, or mainly source it after a customer places an order?”

**If they buy stock:** “What normally rules one out for you first: purchase price, specification or delivery timing?” Listen, then answer the actual issue. If price is the blocker and there is no margin, acknowledge it rather than pushing for a meeting.

**Meeting request:** “That sounds worth checking properly. Would 12 minutes tomorrow at [time and zone] or [alternative] suit? We can compare the build sheet, invoice basis and delivery cost. If it doesn't fit, we'll establish what would.”

**Close the appointment:** “What's the best address for the invite and the one-page car details? Is it okay if I send those? Will you be the person approving the purchase, or should someone else join?”

**If no car is confirmed:** “I'm mapping the buying requirements for this model before sending offers. Do you buy into stock or only against an order?” Do not reuse the opening's availability and price claims.

### Cold call 2 Chauffeur fleet

**Aim:** learn the replacement cycle and book a fleet-requirements call, using S-Class or 7 Series as the relevant category.

**Opening:** “Hi [name], Max from Optimum Prague. Your fleet page shows [verified S-Class/7 Series model]. Is vehicle replacement something you handle, or is there a fleet buyer I should speak with?”

**Reason:** “I'm looking at whether our [verified car or authorised sourcing category] could fit operators replacing their executive cars. Before I send anything, do you buy those vehicles yourselves, lease them, or use partner vehicles?”

**Qualification:** “Are any replacements or additional cars planned in the next three to six months? And which two specifications are non-negotiable for your service?”

**If they mention rear comfort and black paint:** “Understood. We would also need to verify the actual seating configuration, luggage space and your local operating requirements. Do you use a maximum age or mileage rule?”

### Gatekeeper and objection responses

**Reception:** “I'm trying to reach whoever buys vehicles for your stock or fleet. This is about [specific model/category] supply. Is that the owner, a buyer or a central purchasing team?” Be transparent; do not pretend to know the owner.

**Too busy:** “Understood. Is there a better time for a short call, or should I leave it?” If they propose a time, confirm it. Do not keep pitching over them.

**Send me information:** “Happy to, if you'd like it. Which model and price basis should I make it relevant to? I can send one page. Would you prefer to reply when ready, or agree a short follow-up?” This converts a vague brush-off into a useful requirement without forcing a meeting.

**We already have suppliers:** “That makes sense. Are there particular specifications or delivery dates they sometimes cannot cover? If everything is covered, there may be no reason to change anything.” Offer an occasional authorised alternative only if welcomed.

**Your price is too high:** “What acquisition level would leave room after your transport and preparation costs, assuming the specification and VAT basis match?” Acknowledge a real gap. Do not promise an unapproved discount or compare a margin-scheme car with a VAT-deductible one as though they were identical.

**I don't know your company:** “Fair point. I can send our company details through an agreed channel and arrange a live vehicle check or independent inspection. What verification does your purchasing process require?” Only offer evidence and inspection access the company can actually provide.

**Not the right person:** “Thanks. Which role handles purchases, and is there a published contact route you recommend?” If they voluntarily name someone, verify the role. An internal referral is not blanket marketing permission.

**We are not buying / do not contact us:** “Understood. I'll update our records. Thank you.” Record the objection and suppress further marketing through the relevant channels.

## 5 Lessons from experienced car sellers

### Tom Hartley and The Dealmaker

The verified book is **Tom Hartley — The Dealmaker**, an autobiography written with journalist Ken Gibson. The publisher's catalogue confirms that identity. This research did not access the complete book, so the lessons below are attributed to Hartley's public interviews and book discussion, not to invented chapters, page numbers or quotations from the book. Tom Hartley here means the senior dealer, not Tom Hartley Jnr. [Publisher catalogue](https://www.veloce.co.uk/warehouse/downloads/catalogues/Veloce-Automotive-Books-March-2024.pdf)

**Reputation compounds through consistent treatment.** In a 2023 Car Dealer interview, Hartley describes treating customers consistently across very different transaction sizes and a reputation earned over decades. **Application:** give a small independent dealer the same accurate documents and follow-through as a major collector. Make every claim easy to verify; refer uncertainty to the right person instead of improvising. [Hartley interview](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/exclusive-video-hypercar-dealer-tom-hartley-on-his-secret-to-selling-supercars/284368)

**React to the market rather than defend an old asking price.** In the JBR interview, Hartley discusses responding quickly to changing values and treating cars commercially. **Application:** refresh comparable cars and delivery economics before each offer. Preserve genuine differences in specification, VAT and condition. Do not confuse an ambitious advertised retail price with an executable trade bid.

**Understand the person and the requirement.** In the same interview, Hartley discusses communicating across different customer backgrounds and reading their needs. **Application:** ask the dealer what sells, the chauffeur what clients require and the rental operator what earns a return. Let those answers determine your presentation. These applications are this playbook's recommendations, not verbatim book teachings. [JBR interview and full transcript](https://jbrcapital.com/selig-stretton-fund-your-passion-podcast/episode-5-with-tom-hartley/)

**Use the book as a case study in judgement.** An established trader's willingness to commit capital depends on experience, cash, relationships and risk tolerance. As an employed salesperson, work within your buying, pricing and deposit authority. Hartley's biography is not evidence that a new hire can achieve a particular commission in three months.

### Joe Verde on finding the real requirement

Joe Verde's published training sample stresses asking about how a customer will use a vehicle before choosing the presentation and explaining value. **Application to B2B:** replace the retail questions with model turnover, holding period, passenger requirements, mileage, replacement timing and funding. Then connect each important option to a use case. Rear comfort may matter to a chauffeur; a difficult colour or unusual seating configuration may make the same car less saleable to a dealer. [Joe Verde training sample](https://store.joeverde.com/assets/pdf/snl_sample.pdf)

The scripts in this document apply that question-led approach while preserving transparent pricing and the buyer's freedom to decline. They do not adopt every retail closing tactic. A trade buyer who asks for a price or document should receive the confirmed information promptly.

## 6 Activity and income planning

### What 300 calls a day would require

Treat 300 as **attempts**, not conversations, and test the capacity before making it a commitment. Illustrative assumptions: 30 connected calls at six minutes each require 180 minutes; 270 unsuccessful attempts at 45 seconds each require 202.5 minutes. That is **6 hours 22.5 minutes** before research, callbacks, meeting time, notes, documents or negotiation. These are arithmetic assumptions, not observed automotive conversion rates.

At 20 working days, 300 attempts daily means 6,000 attempts monthly. If each account averages three permitted attempts, you need roughly 2,000 account cycles per month. That does not establish that 2,000 suitable and contactable buyers exist for your specific cars, territories and language capacity.

Start with a two-week pilot of 50 well-matched businesses and ten verified offers or authorised sourcing categories. Compare list quality, actual buyer conversations, requested dossiers, held meetings, qualified offers, deposits and delivered gross profit. Do not scale bad records just because a dialler can process them quickly.

The earlier memo's 30–60 calls, 10–20 conversations and other quotas are retained as historical suggestions in section 8. They were not measured benchmarks. Set a sustainable daily activity level from your observed research time, connection rate and follow-up load.

### A working day once the process is established

- Begin with stock, price and delivery confirmations; remove cars that are unavailable or have unresolved material facts.
- Research a focused batch of accounts and review the day's permitted outreach routes.
- Run calling blocks in the buyers' local business hours; update records immediately after meaningful conversations.
- Send requested dossiers and hold short qualification meetings.
- Work the operational steps on live deals: documents, inspection, finance, delivery and payment status.
- Finish by recording tomorrow's next actions and checking which deposits or deliveries affect commission eligibility.

### Keep the income target separate from revenue

Your stated goal is **€10,000 monthly take-home**. That differs from €10,000 gross commission, €10,000 salary plus commission before tax, and €10,000 dealership profit. This document cannot estimate the required gross pay accurately without your employment/tax position and Optimum's actual plan.

For scale only, if commission were a flat **€500 gross per delivered car**, €10,000 gross commission would require 20 delivered cars; at **€1,000**, ten; at **€2,000**, five. These are invented illustration rates, not Optimum pay estimates. If commission were 10% of eligible dealer gross profit, €10,000 gross commission would require €100,000 of that eligible profit. A €200,000 car does not imply a €20,000 commission base.

Obtain the actual rules: eligible margin definition, flat fee or percentage, caps, splits, repeat customers, self-sourced deal credit, cancellation clawbacks, when payment is earned, and whether deposits or only collected deliveries count. Late-year order slots might not pay commission before year-end. No reliable income promise follows from call volume alone.

## 7 Interview proposal and implementation brief

### The proposal to bring into the interview

“I want to test a repeatable European buyer-development process. I would start with ten verified vehicles or sourcing categories and 50 relevant dealers or fleet operators. Each account would have a source, a reason to buy, the correct purchasing role and an approved contact route. The aim of the first call is a short review or a clear buying brief. I would report qualified requirements, requested dossiers, held meetings, offers and delivered gross profit. Once the data and conversion justify it, I would automate the permitted research steps.”

Bring one example dealer record, one chauffeur buying brief and one vehicle dossier using clearly labelled public or hypothetical facts. Show the source links and the unresolved questions. That demonstrates your method without claiming to have customers or supplier access you do not have.

### Questions that determine whether the plan can work

- Which cars are owned, consigned, brokered or order slots, and who confirms availability?
- Can I source new supply against a buyer requirement, or sell only assigned stock?
- Which countries, languages, accounts and channels are open to me? How are existing relationships protected?
- What pricing authority, minimum margin, transport support and finance support will I have?
- Can we handle a matched pair or batch, and who commits to specification and delivery?
- What happens to commission on a self-sourced buyer, repeat sale, split deal, cancellation or delivery after year-end?
- What prospecting data and outreach processes does the company already use, and what can I improve without duplicating them?

### Requirements for the future tool

Build this only after a manual pilot shows which records lead to useful conversations. It should produce a reviewable research queue, not automatically contact every company it finds.

**Source record:** source name, URL/API, licence or access basis, allowed fields, storage restrictions, refresh schedule and last review. Keep a list of approved sources so adding a new website is a deliberate decision.

**Company record:** legal name, trading name, registration identifier, country, city, domain, parent/branch, buyer category, model evidence and ownership/purchasing status. Keep unknown values as unknown.

**Contact record:** published name and role, office phone, email, preferred channel, evidence link/date, language, verification status, outreach basis and suppression status. Do not require a personal mobile if an office route works.

**Buying brief:** model/generation, must-have options, acceptable variations, age/mileage, quantity, budget and VAT basis, destination, finance route, replacement date, sign-off role and agreed follow-up.

**Vehicle and opportunity record:** VIN/allocation, confirmed status, price validity, exact match, landed-cost assumptions, documents outstanding, next step, deal owner, gross-profit basis and commission status. Keep one record per real opportunity so duplicate adverts do not inflate pipeline.

**Useful AI tasks:** classify permitted source text; translate it for research; extract fields with evidence; highlight contradictions; suggest matches; prepare a draft dossier or message for human review. **Human decisions:** confirm identity, buying intent, permitted outreach, vehicle facts, price, tax treatment and promises.

**Quality checks:** sample records for false positives; measure duplicate branches, dead websites, wrong roles and missing provenance; track which sources produce actual qualified buyers. Respect rate limits, deletion/objection handling and provider-specific retention restrictions. Do not bypass logins, CAPTCHAs or technical restrictions.

### Three experiments beyond a generic call list

**Replacement calendar.** Ask willing fleet buyers for their next replacement month and specification. A relevant reminder three months before replacement can outperform a large random list. The calendar is useful only when dates come from buyers or clearly identified evidence.

**Two-sided trade requirement.** When a fleet replaces cars, ask whether its outgoing vehicles need an approved remarketing route. That may reveal both a replacement purchase and a disposal opportunity. Do not promise valuations, finance settlement, buybacks or intermediary fees without authority.

**Missing specification alerts.** With an opted-in dealer, record the two or three specifications they struggle to obtain. Send an alert only when a verified car fits. If permitted inventory monitoring shows a listing disappearing, ask whether it sold and needs replacement; never automatically label deletion as a sale or infer sales speed from it.

### European fleet buyers and a better outbound system for luxury cars

**Research date:** 26 September 2026  
**Inventory used:** the 38 Optimum Prague mobile.de adverts already audited  
**Purpose:** identify repeat-purchase businesses and explain how a tech-style outbound system should be adapted to high-value vehicle trading.

### The answer first

There is a fleet market, but it is narrower and more operational than a SaaS market. A chauffeur or rental business does not buy because a car looks interesting on a demo. It buys when the vehicle fits a service class, can be financed and insured, has the right tax basis, arrives on a dependable date, and can earn or preserve enough money to justify the acquisition.

The best first targets are independent operators with roughly 5–50 premium vehicles, not the largest rental brands. They can make a quick owner or fleet-manager decision, buy one or two cars when a gap appears, and often have public evidence of exactly the models Optimum carries. Large platforms such as SIXT, Europcar, Blacklane and Wheely are useful market signals or referral channels, but their procurement is centralised or their vehicles are supplied by partners. They are weak first targets for a Prague salesperson trying to place one specific VIN.

The commercial unit should be a **verified car-to-buyer match**. Each opportunity needs a car, a destination, a buyer, a landed-cost view and a dated next step. That changes the work from “fill a generic pipeline” to “find the few businesses that can use this exact vehicle now.”

The B targets may buy, lease, take consignment or refer you to their supplier network. The C targets have purchasing power but are usually governed by central fleet, finance and remarketing contracts, so a local salesperson should not spend the first month treating them like owner-managed dealers.

### How luxury-car outbound differs from tech outbound

| Tech-sales habit | Luxury-vehicle reality | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build a large account list, map personas and run a sequence | The account may be irrelevant unless it can use the exact car, tax basis and delivery date | Start with the car and work backwards to matching accounts |
| Lead with a problem and book a demo | There is no generic demo; the buyer needs proof of a real unit | Use a ten-minute stock-qualification call, then a one-page vehicle dossier or live walkaround |
| Sell recurring software value | A car is a one-off asset with depreciation, finance, insurance, tax and residual risk | Lead with landed cost, utilisation, replacement timing and resale path |
| Many stakeholders can be nurtured over weeks | A dealer owner or fleet director may decide quickly if the unit is right, while logistics/finance can still stop it | Find the economic buyer early, then surface document and funding blockers |
| A product can be sold repeatedly without inventory constraints | Each VIN, option set and location is finite; stale adverts and order slots are common | Keep a verified status field and an expiry date on every offer |
| Activity volume is a useful proxy for pipeline health | 300 generic calls can create noise and damage trust; one correct buyer can matter more than 1,000 irrelevant contacts | Measure verified matches, qualified quotes, deposits, delivered gross profit and commission |
| Demo-to-close is the core funnel | Inspection, invoice/VAT, warranty, transport, registration and payment are part of the close | Treat operations as part of sales, not hand-off work after the “yes” |
| A contact database is the centre of the system | A vehicle-to-buyer graph is more useful than a company list | Store buyer requirements, model gaps, fleet size, replacement month, tax country and last verified source |


The first-call question is therefore not “Would you like to see our range?” It is: **“I have a verified [model/specification] that may fit the class you already operate. Do you buy this class directly, lease it, or source it only against a client order?”** That question quickly separates owners, lessees, consignment businesses and people who cannot buy.

### The better system: a vehicle-to-buyer matching desk

#### 1. Create a car record before creating a lead

For every Optimum advert, capture the VIN or build sheet when available, make/model, generation, powertrain, seats, colour/interior, option codes, mileage, physical location, selling entity, VAT status, warranty, delivery date, gross/net price, transport estimate and an expiry timestamp. Keep the public mobile.de URL as evidence, but mark every important field as **confirmed**, **seller-claimed** or **unverified**.

The existing 38-ad audit shows why this matters: the Q9 has conflicting fuel fields, several X5 adverts conflict on generation/engine, S-Class adverts conflict on seat count or power, and “in stock” sometimes means an order slot. A clean record is a selling advantage because it prevents sending a buyer a wrong offer.

#### 2. Build the account universe from buying signals

Use public sources and approved business data to find companies with at least two of these signals:

- a fleet page showing S-Class, 7-Series, G63, Maybach, Range Rover, X7 or similar;
- multiple matching vehicles in current inventory or a booking engine with the class available;
- a “buy a car”, “sell your car”, trade-in, procurement or import-to-order page;
- several cities, hotel/event contracts, corporate roadshows, security work or concierge services;
- a visible replacement pattern, new-model launch, expansion to another city or hiring for drivers/operations;
- recent “sold”, “new arrival”, “coming soon” or reservation activity on the company website.

Google Maps and the company website are useful for the account itself. LinkedIn is useful for manually finding the owner, managing director, fleet manager, procurement lead or operations director. Use a licensed data provider if Optimum approves one. Do not build the system around scraping LinkedIn or automating connection requests: LinkedIn’s User Agreement prohibits scraping/copying and unauthorised automation, and its help centre states that crawlers, bots and extensions are not permitted. [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) · [LinkedIn prohibited software](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibited-software-and-extensions)

#### 3. Score a match, not a person

Use a simple score that can be explained to a manager:

| Signal | Points | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Exact model or same service class | 30 | Current fleet/listing page or written requirement |
| Repeat-fleet potential | 20 | Five or more relevant cars, multiple cities or recurring contracts |
| Buying route | 20 | Explicit buy/sell/import/replace/procurement route |
| Country economics | 15 | VAT, registration tax, transport and warranty are workable |
| Freshness | 10 | Current page, new arrival, expansion or replacement signal |
| Contactability | 5 | Public phone/email and identifiable business role |


Only the top-scoring accounts receive a specific vehicle offer. The score is a prioritisation tool, not a claim that a company will buy.

#### 4. Use a short, car-specific sequence

For a qualified business:

1. Call the public business number and ask for the owner, fleet buyer or operations lead. State the exact vehicle and why it may fit their fleet.
2. Send a concise email with one vehicle, not a catalogue: price basis, VAT, mileage, location, delivery date, options, warranty and the one unresolved item.
3. Use LinkedIn manually to confirm the role and make a relevant connection. Do not automate connection requests or scrape phone numbers.
4. Call again with a single qualification question: “If the build sheet and landed cost match, is this something you could buy or place with a client?”
5. If positive, send the full dossier and arrange a video walkaround, inspection or partner viewing. Ask for a written requirement, purchase route and timing.
6. Close the operational path: pro-forma invoice, deposit, finance/transport, documentation, handover and commission attribution.

This replaces the SaaS demo with a **verified-vehicle review**. The call is short, specific and useful even if the answer is no.

#### 5. Create a small allocation bulletin

Do not blast 38 adverts to every company. Create an opt-in weekly bulletin for qualified businesses with five to eight verified cars grouped by buyer use case:

- “Executive cabin”: S450/S580e and BMW 740/740d with rear comfort;
- “High-end SUV”: G63, GLS, X7, Range Rover;
- “Performance and specialist”: RS5, ALPINA, Ferrari and modified G-Class;
- “Order slots”: cars with confirmed build and delivery dates.

Each bulletin should show the evidence date and a clear “available / slot / seller confirmation pending” status. The point is to become a useful allocation desk, not another person sending generic stock lists.

#### 6. Add referral loops around the fleet

The fastest route to a fleet buyer may be a business that does not buy the car itself. Ask for introductions from:

- chauffeur networks and partner platforms such as Blacklane and Wheely;
- luxury hotels, private aviation handlers, concierge companies and event agencies;
- security and close-protection companies that need G-Class, S-Class or discreet SUVs;
- specialist bodybuilders, armourers and conversion companies;
- independent leasing brokers and fleet remarketing firms;
- dealers with an outgoing vehicle and a customer waiting for an upgrade.

Paying referral fees or sharing customer data needs Optimum’s approval and a written process. The commercial idea is a permission-based referral network, not hidden commission or uncontrolled data sharing.

### Is 300 calls a day realistic?

**Update for this edition.** The figures below were proposed targets, not measured benchmarks. Use section 6 for the current capacity calculation and pilot recommendation.


Three hundred **dial attempts** a day is possible with a clean list, a dialler and very short calls. Three hundred thoughtful conversations is not realistic for this market, and it is not the right goal. A luxury-car buyer often needs a VIN, equipment list, VAT treatment and delivery answer before the conversation has value.

A sensible first operating target is:

- 40–80 new accounts researched per day;
- 30–60 high-fit call attempts, plus follow-ups;
- 10–20 real conversations;
- 5–10 verified car-to-buyer matches added to the pipeline;
- 3–8 complete offers per week;
- a measured number of deposits, delivered vehicles and commission paid.

After the data and talk track work, an assistant or dialler can raise attempts to 150–300 per day. Keep the high-value research and qualification human. If 300 attempts produce no qualified quote, the answer is better targeting, not more dials.

Use these funnel fields: **account found → role verified → relevant vehicle class → contact made → requirement confirmed → vehicle verified → offer sent → documents accepted → deposit → delivered → commission paid**. This gives you the same discipline as tech sales while measuring the actual automotive bottlenecks.

### Email, calls and personal data

**Update for this edition.** The more specific guidance in section 3 applies. In particular, a public email address, role inbox or GDPR legitimate-interest assessment does not by itself make Czech cold marketing email permissible.


The European Commission says professional business email addresses and business phone numbers can still be personal data when they identify an individual. Direct marketing may sometimes rely on legitimate interest, but the business must meet GDPR requirements, follow the ePrivacy rules for electronic marketing, inform people and honour objections. [European Commission: legal grounds](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en) · [European Commission: direct-marketing objections](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/dealing-requests-individuals_en)

For the interview, frame this as good sales hygiene: start with public company numbers and role accounts, identify yourself and Optimum, keep a suppression list, stop when someone objects, and ask the company which countries and channels it approves. The goal is an effective system that can survive scrutiny, not a scraping project that gets accounts or domains blocked.

### The interview proposal

Bring one concrete pilot instead of promising “AI will create unlimited volume”:

> “I mapped the public stock by model, specification, status and likely buyer class. I would run a two-week verified-matching pilot on ten cars: build a ranked list of 50 fleet and dealer accounts, identify the real fleet or trade buyer, send only evidence-backed offers, and report replies, qualified requirements, offers, deposits and delivered gross profit. If it works, we automate the repetitive research and keep the final qualification human.”

That pitch shows the company three things: you understand that inventory and documentation matter, you can create a repeatable pipeline, and you are willing to prove the method with numbers. It also gives you a defensible route toward the €10,000 monthly target, because the commission path is tied to delivered gross profit rather than activity theatre.

## 9 Luxury car sales glossary

Use this as a working reference. Equipment names, legal treatment and contract terms vary by model, year and country. For a live offer, the build sheet, official vehicle documents and agreed contract control. Definitions explain the language; they do not establish that an advertised car has the feature or qualifies for a tax treatment.

### People and trading models

**OEM** — Original equipment manufacturer; in this context, the vehicle maker. “OEM part” usually means an original manufacturer part rather than an aftermarket replacement.

**Franchised or authorised dealer** — A business appointed to represent a manufacturer under an agreement. Its freedom to source outside vehicles depends on its business model and contracts.

**Independent dealer** — A dealer operating outside a particular manufacturer's authorised sales network; may specialise in several prestige brands.

**Dealer principal** — The person leading a dealership, often its owner or senior operator. This person may delegate purchasing.

**Vehicle buyer or stock buyer** — The person responsible for purchasing cars for resale. Often a more relevant contact than a general sales manager.

**Fleet manager** — The person managing business vehicles, their use, replacement and costs. They may specify cars without signing purchase contracts.

**Procurement** — The business function that selects suppliers and negotiates purchases under the company's rules.

**Economic buyer** — The person or function controlling the money and commercial approval. Distinguish this from the day-to-day user.

**Wholesaler or trade dealer** — A business buying and selling mainly to other vehicle businesses, usually with different margins and obligations from retail.

**Retail buyer** — The final customer buying for use, rather than a dealer buying for resale. A business can also be an end user.

**Broker** — An intermediary arranging a transaction. Establish who owns the car, invoices the buyer and carries contractual obligations.

**Sourcing agent** — A person or company finding a vehicle to a buyer's brief, sometimes for a disclosed fee or agreed margin.

**Consignment** — A dealer markets a car for its owner under agreed terms. The advert does not prove dealer ownership or authority to accept every offer.

**Sale or return** — An arrangement allowing a dealer to market or hold a car with a contractual right to return it if unsold. Exact terms matter.

**Buying mandate** — Authority to seek or buy a vehicle within stated parameters. A casual expression of interest is not necessarily a mandate.

**Back-to-back trade** — Linked purchase and resale transactions, often arranged close together. Cancellation, payment and timing risks remain on both sides.

**Parallel import** — A genuine vehicle supplied through a distribution route outside the destination's usual authorised channel. Check specification, warranty and registration suitability.

**Chauffeur operator** — A business providing a vehicle with a professional driver. It may own, lease or subcontract the vehicles shown.

**NCC** — Italian “noleggio con conducente,” hire with a driver. Operating and licensing requirements need local confirmation.

**VTC** — A term used in markets such as France and Spain for regulated private-hire services with a driver; the precise legal category differs by country.

**Affiliate or partner fleet** — Vehicles supplied by another operator in a service network. The network's total capacity is not the same as vehicles owned by one company.

### Identity and specification

**VIN** — Vehicle identification number, normally 17 characters on modern production cars. Match it across the car and documents.

**Build sheet or data card** — Manufacturer-derived specification for an individual car, often including option codes. Confirm that it belongs to the offered VIN.

**Option code** — A manufacturer's identifier for a feature or package. Decode using the correct model/year rather than a generic list.

**Model year or MY** — A manufacturer's model-year designation, which may differ from calendar build year and first registration year.

**Build date** — When a vehicle was manufactured. It does not establish when warranty or registration began.

**First registration** — The date the car was first registered for road use. This can affect warranty, value and tax treatment.

**Generation or chassis code** — The manufacturer's designation for a model generation/body variant. Similar badges across generations can describe very different cars.

**Facelift or LCI** — A mid-cycle model update; BMW uses LCI for Life Cycle Impulse. Determine what actually changed on the relevant car.

**Pre-registered** — Registered before sale to the eventual user, sometimes by a dealer. Low mileage does not mean unregistered or a full untouched warranty term.

**Demonstrator or demo** — A car used for test drives or display. Verify mileage, use, registration and warranty start.

**Delivery mileage** — Low mileage associated with manufacturing, transport or preparation. It is not a universal fixed number or proof of no prior use.

**LHD and RHD** — Left-hand drive and right-hand drive, referring to steering-wheel position. Not the side of the road on which the country drives.

**LWB and SWB** — Long and short wheelbase. Longer wheelbase may improve rear space, but confirm the actual dimensions and seating.

**Saloon or sedan** — A passenger-car body style with a separate boot/trunk. Common in executive chauffeur fleets.

**SUV** — Sport utility vehicle. The label alone says little about luggage room, off-road ability or passenger seating.

**Coupé** — A body-style/marketing designation commonly associated with a sporting roofline. Some manufacturers use it for four-door cars or SUVs.

**Homologated seating** — The number/configuration of seats officially approved for the vehicle. An executive rear console can reduce passenger capacity.

**EU specification or market specification** — The configuration intended for a particular market. An advert's “EU spec” claim still requires document and equipment checks.

**Factory option** — Equipment fitted or specified by the manufacturer during production. Distinguish it from later modifications.

**Retrofit** — Equipment installed after manufacture. Check compatibility, documentation, coding and warranty effects.

### Engines and drivetrains

**ICE** — Internal-combustion engine, usually petrol or diesel. An ICE may also form part of a hybrid powertrain.

**MHEV or mild hybrid** — A combustion car with electrical assistance. Do not assume it can drive useful distances solely on electricity or charge from a plug.

**HEV or full hybrid** — A hybrid with a combustion engine and electric drive, typically without external charging. Capabilities vary.

**PHEV** — Plug-in hybrid electric vehicle. It has a combustion engine and externally rechargeable battery; it is not a pure electric car.

**BEV** — Battery electric vehicle, driven solely by electric motors using a rechargeable battery.

**V8 and V12** — Engine layouts with eight or twelve cylinders in two banks. They do not by themselves define power or reliability.

**Turbocharged** — Uses exhaust energy to drive a compressor that increases intake pressure. “Twin turbo” generally refers to two turbochargers.

**Displacement** — Engine capacity, usually litres or cubic centimetres. A modern badge number may not equal engine displacement.

**kW** — Kilowatts, a unit of power. Used for engine/motor output and charging power; context matters.

**PS, hp and bhp** — Horsepower conventions. Metric PS and mechanical horsepower differ slightly; preserve the stated unit when comparing specifications.

**Nm or torque** — Newton-metres, the usual torque unit. Torque and power are related but are not interchangeable measures.

**AWD and 4WD** — All-wheel/four-wheel drive systems. Their architecture and operating modes vary; do not imply off-road capability from the label alone.

**4MATIC, xDrive and quattro** — Mercedes-Benz, BMW and Audi drivetrain names associated with all-wheel drive. Verify the exact variant.

**RWD and FWD** — Rear-wheel drive and front-wheel drive.

**WLTP** — A standardised vehicle test procedure used for measures such as consumption, CO₂ and electric range. Real-world results vary.

**Euro emissions standard** — A regulated emissions classification, such as a particular Euro 6 stage. Different stages and local access rules matter.

**CO₂ figure** — Certified carbon-dioxide emissions, generally stated in grams per kilometre. It can affect taxes and registration costs.

**kWh** — Kilowatt-hours, a unit of energy used for battery capacity or energy consumption. It is not the same as charging power in kW.

**Battery state of health or SOH** — A measure or estimate of battery condition relative to its original capacity/performance. Identify the test method and date.

**AC and DC charging** — Alternating-current and direct-current charging. Vehicle hardware limits, supply and battery conditions affect actual charging speed.

### Luxury brands and equipment language

**Mercedes-AMG versus AMG Line** — AMG denotes performance models/branding; AMG Line is an equipment or styling designation and does not make a regular model a full AMG performance car.

**BMW M versus M Sport** — BMW M includes performance models; M Sport commonly describes an equipment/styling package. M Performance variants sit in another part of the range. Use the exact model designation.

**Audi RS versus S line** — RS identifies high-performance models; S line generally describes an equipment/styling package. Audi S models are also distinct.

**Mercedes-Maybach** — A specific ultra-luxury model/brand designation. A highly equipped S-Class or an S580e badge alone does not establish Maybach identity.

**BMW ALPINA** — A distinct designation associated with ALPINA-developed BMW-based vehicles. Verify identity and documentation; badges and wheels alone are insufficient.

**BRABUS and MANSORY** — Specialist tuning/conversion brands. Establish whether the car has genuine documented components, a complete conversion or merely cosmetic imitation.

**MANUFAKTUR and BMW Individual** — Manufacturer personalisation labels/programmes. Confirm the actual paint, trim and option codes rather than assuming every feature is bespoke.

**Nappa and Merino leather** — Leather/trim designations used in luxury interiors. Scope, treatment and coverage vary; “leather interior” does not necessarily mean every surface is leather.

**Executive rear seating** — A broad description of comfort-focused rear seats, potentially including recline, ventilation or massage. Check each function and passenger capacity.

**Rear entertainment** — Screens/media functions for rear passengers. Equipment, subscriptions and regional compatibility vary.

**Soft-close doors** — A mechanism that pulls an almost-closed door into its latched position. It is not the same as fully powered door opening.

**Air suspension** — Suspension using air springs. Relevant to comfort, load management and future maintenance cost.

**Rear-wheel steering** — A system that turns the rear wheels to improve manoeuvrability or stability. Check whether it is fitted and activated.

**Panoramic roof** — A large glazed roof; it may be fixed or partly opening. Verify the version and condition.

**HUD** — Head-up display, projecting selected information into the driver's forward view.

**ADAS** — Advanced driver-assistance systems, such as lane assistance or adaptive cruise. They do not make a vehicle autonomous.

**ACC** — Adaptive cruise control, which can adjust speed to traffic within system limits. Functions vary by model.

**Carbon-ceramic brakes** — High-performance brake components with different operating and replacement characteristics from conventional brakes. Inspect condition and replacement cost.

**PPF** — Paint protection film. Film may help protect paint but is not proof of original paint or accident-free history.

**Wrap** — A film covering that changes a car's appearance. Record the underlying paint colour and inspect its condition where appropriate.

**Ceramic coating** — A protective surface treatment. It is not the same as PPF and does not eliminate maintenance or inspection needs.

**Armoured vehicle** — A vehicle with protective modifications. An ordinary G63 or security escort car is not automatically armoured; verify the claimed certification and weight implications.

### History and condition

**Provenance** — The documented history and origin of a vehicle, including ownership, servicing and significant events.

**FSH or full service history** — A claim that service records are complete. Check actual dates, mileage, work and whether the maker's schedule was followed.

**Manufacturer warranty** — Coverage provided under the maker's terms. Confirm remaining time/mileage, start date, transferability and territorial conditions.

**Third-party warranty** — Coverage from an external provider. Exclusions, claim limits and approved repair routes may differ substantially.

**CPO or approved used** — A manufacturer/dealer certified-used programme. Requirements and coverage are programme-specific.

**PDI** — Pre-delivery inspection, the checks before handover. It is not a substitute for an independent purchase inspection where needed.

**PPI** — Pre-purchase inspection by a suitable independent specialist. Agree scope; no inspection guarantees discovery of every defect.

**Accident-free** — A history claim requiring a clear definition and supporting evidence. Cosmetic repair, replaced panels and structural damage should be described accurately.

**Original paint** — A claim that relevant panels retain factory paint. Use inspection and evidence; an advert statement is not proof.

**Reconditioning or preparation** — Work required to bring the car to the agreed sales standard, including service, repair, tyres, detailing and cosmetic work.

**Outstanding finance or lien** — A lender/security interest that may need discharge before clear transfer. Verify the relevant country's process.

**Recall** — A manufacturer-required corrective action for affected vehicles. Check whether a particular VIN has open campaigns.

**Roadworthiness test** — A statutory vehicle inspection, such as Czech STK, German HU or UK MOT. Passing one is not a comprehensive condition guarantee or cross-border registration approval.

### Pricing and profitability

**Asking price** — The advertised amount, which may differ from the final negotiated transaction price.

**Trade price** — A business-to-business price offered to the motor trade. It is not automatically net of VAT or below every retail price.

**Retail price** — The price to the end customer; compare on a consistent tax and fee basis.

**Bid** — A buyer's offered purchase price, subject to any conditions. An indicative valuation is not necessarily a binding bid.

**Spread** — The gap between buying and selling prices. Costs and tax treatment can make it different from actual profit.

**Gross profit** — Revenue less the direct costs included in the business's definition. Ask exactly which costs Optimum deducts for commission purposes.

**Margin** — Often gross profit divided by selling price. Specify whether you mean a percentage or an amount.

**Markup** — Usually profit uplift relative to cost. Buying at 100 and selling at 120 is a 20% markup but a 16.7% margin, before other costs.

**Landed cost** — Total cost to acquire and bring a car to a stated location/status. Specify whether transport, tax, registration, preparation and finance are included.

**Net vehicle price** — Usually a price excluding VAT in this context. “Net to seller” can instead mean proceeds after fees; always clarify.

**Gross vehicle price** — Usually a price including applicable VAT. It does not necessarily include all destination costs.

**OTR or on-the-road price** — A price including specified items needed to put a vehicle on the road. Included taxes and fees vary by market and offer.

**Stock turn** — How often stock is sold and replaced over a period. Define how it is measured; advert changes do not prove sales.

**Days in stock** — Time a business has held a vehicle. This may differ from how long an advert has been visible.

**Stocking finance or floorplan** — Finance used to fund dealer inventory. Interest and repayment deadlines affect the cost of holding a car.

**Working capital** — Money tied up in stock and day-to-day operations, including payment timing and recoverable taxes.

**Depreciation** — Loss in a vehicle's value over time or use. It can vary sharply by specification, condition and market.

**Residual value or RV** — Expected or actual value at the end of a holding period. A forecast is not a guaranteed buyback.

**TCO** — Total cost of ownership over a defined period, including relevant depreciation, funding, maintenance, energy, insurance and taxes.

**Utilisation** — How much a fleet asset is used or earning revenue relative to available capacity. State whether measured by days, hours or distance.

**Downtime** — Time a vehicle is unavailable for revenue-producing or operational use, including repair and delivery delays.

**Remarketing** — Reselling vehicles, often after a lease, rental or fleet holding period.

**Defleeting** — Removing a vehicle from an operational fleet and preparing it for return or sale.

**Buyback** — A contractual repurchase commitment, often subject to mileage, condition and timing limits. Never imply one without agreed terms.

### Tax and cross border documents

The VAT definitions below use the European Commission's explanation of buying and selling cars. A live B2B invoice may involve conditions beyond this introductory glossary. [European Commission car VAT guidance](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/buying-and-selling-cars_en)

**VAT** — Value added tax. Treatment depends on the transaction, vehicle, parties and destination; it is not simply removed because a buyer is foreign.

**VAT deductible or VAT qualifying** — Trade shorthand indicating that VAT may be separately invoiced and potentially recoverable by an eligible buyer. Recovery still depends on the buyer's circumstances and local rules.

**Margin scheme** — A special VAT treatment for eligible second-hand goods, generally based on the dealer's margin. VAT is not shown as an ordinary separately recoverable amount for the buyer.

**New means of transport for EU VAT** — A car is new for this purpose if supplied within six months of first use OR if it has travelled no more than 6,000 km. A car marketed as “used” can still qualify.

**Used for that EU VAT age and mileage test** — More than six months since first use AND more than 6,000 km. This does not by itself determine every other VAT obligation.

**Intra-community supply and acquisition** — A supply and corresponding acquisition across EU member states. Any exemption/zero-rating and buyer accounting depend on the applicable conditions and evidence.

**VIES** — The EU system for checking VAT-number validity for intra-EU transactions. A valid result is not a complete fraud, solvency or transaction-tax check. [Official VIES guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/finance-and-tax/vat/check-vat-number-vies/index_en.htm)

**IČO and DIČ** — Czech business identification number and tax identification number respectively. A DIČ and a valid VAT registration should not be assumed to mean the same thing in every situation.

**Registration tax** — A destination charge connected with registration, separate from VAT. Some markets tie it to emissions or other vehicle characteristics.

**Customs duty and import VAT** — Potential charges when crossing a customs/tax border. Europe is not a single customs jurisdiction; confirm origin, destination and applicable arrangements.

**EORI** — An identifier used by economic operators in EU customs dealings. It is not the same as a VAT number.

**CoC** — Certificate of Conformity, documenting conformity with the relevant vehicle type approval. It is not proof of ownership or a guarantee that every local registration step is complete.

**Homologation** — Approval that a vehicle/type meets applicable technical requirements. Non-standard or modified vehicles may need additional checks.

**Registration certificate** — Official vehicle-registration documentation. Its ownership significance varies by country; do not treat it alone as complete title evidence.

**Export plates** — Temporary plates for specified export/transit use. Validity, permitted destinations and insurance must be checked.

**CMR consignment note** — A road-transport document used for international carriage. It records transport information; it is not a substitute for a detailed condition report or insurance review.

**Enclosed transport** — Car transport in an enclosed carrier. Confirm cover, loading method, delivery access and actual insurance limits.

**Incoterms** — Standard ICC rules allocating specified delivery costs, obligations and risks in sales contracts. They do not settle all payment, title or warranty questions. [ICC Incoterms guidance](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/incoterms-2020/)

**EXW** — Ex Works: the seller makes goods available at the named place under the agreed Incoterms rule, with extensive responsibilities on the buyer. Clarify practical loading/export arrangements.

**DAP** — Delivered at Place: seller arranges delivery to the named destination ready for unloading; buyer generally handles import clearance and relevant import charges where applicable.

**DDP** — Delivered Duty Paid: seller takes extensive delivery and import obligations to the named place. Do not offer it without confirming the company can fulfil those obligations.

**ETA** — Estimated time of arrival. An estimate is not a contractual delivery guarantee.

**Lead time** — Time between an agreed trigger, such as an accepted order, and availability or delivery. Define the start and end events.

### Contracts and payment

**Allocation or build slot** — A place in a production/supply schedule. It is not the same as a finished car ready for collection.

**Reservation or hold** — An agreed temporary restriction on offering a vehicle elsewhere. Confirm duration, conditions and who has authority to grant it.

**Pro-forma invoice** — A preliminary invoice-like document setting out proposed details. It is not normally the final VAT invoice or proof of payment.

**Purchase order or PO** — A buyer's order document. Its binding effect depends on terms, acceptance and applicable law.

**Deposit** — An advance payment under agreed terms. Whether it is refundable and what it secures must be explicit.

**Cleared funds** — Money received and available in the agreed account. A payment screenshot is not proof that funds have cleared.

**Escrow** — A third party holds funds under agreed release conditions. Verify the provider and contract; using the word does not make a payment safe.

**KYC** — Know your customer: checks of business/customer identity and relevant transaction facts under the company's legal and risk process.

**Beneficial owner** — The natural person ultimately owning or controlling an entity under the applicable definition. Relevant to transaction checks, not automatically the best sales contact.

**Condition precedent** — A condition that must be met before an obligation or transaction proceeds, such as finance or document approval.

**Handover or acceptance report** — A record of delivery, condition, mileage, keys, accessories and documents accepted or disputed.

**Trade-in or part exchange** — An existing vehicle used as part of the consideration for another car. Its valuation and any finance settlement need separate agreement.

**Sale price versus settlement figure** — The sale price is what the car sells for; a finance settlement figure is what must be paid to discharge a financing obligation.

### Sales activity and commission

**Account** — The business you are targeting. One account can have several contacts and branches.

**Lead** — An initial indication of a potentially relevant account or person. It does not prove buying intent.

**Prospect** — An account considered potentially suitable after initial research. Still not a confirmed buyer.

**ICP** — Ideal customer profile: the characteristics of an account your offer serves well, such as a relevant independent dealer with purchasing authority.

**Buying signal** — Evidence suggesting it is worth asking about demand, such as expansion or a stated requirement. It is not a purchase commitment.

**Buying brief** — The buyer's stated requirements, budget basis, timing and constraints. This is more useful than a generic “interested” tag.

**Qualification** — Establishing real fit, authority, funding route, timing and blockers before investing in a full offer.

**CRM** — Customer relationship management system for accounts, contacts, conversations, opportunities and next steps.

**Pipeline** — The set of open sales opportunities, separated by defined stages. Avoid counting the full retail value as likely income.

**Conversion rate** — The proportion moving between clearly defined stages. Keep the time period and denominator consistent.

**Meeting booked versus meeting held** — An accepted appointment versus an actual conversation. Track both; no-shows do not count as discovery.

**Qualified quote** — An offer to a real buyer with a matching requirement, clear assumptions and a next decision. A mass stock email is not the same thing.

**Deal attribution** — The rules assigning credit for finding or progressing a customer/transaction. Confirm this before several colleagues work the same account.

**Commission base** — The amount to which the commission rate applies, such as eligible gross profit. It may differ from revenue and accounting gross profit.

**Split commission** — Commission shared between people or teams under agreed rules.

**Clawback** — Commission reclaimed or offset after an event such as cancellation, non-payment or reversal, if the plan allows it.

**OTE** — On-target earnings: usually base pay plus variable pay at a defined target. It is not a guaranteed wage or take-home figure.

**Gross pay and take-home pay** — Gross is before employee deductions; take-home is what remains after applicable deductions. Your €10,000 goal is the latter.

**Suppression list** — A record preventing further marketing to contacts/accounts who objected or must otherwise be excluded. Check it before every outreach action.

**Opt-in** — A person's affirmative agreement for a specified communication/use. Record who agreed, to whom, for what and when.

**API, feed and scraper** — An API is a structured access interface; a feed is a supplied stream/file of data; a scraper extracts information from pages. None of these labels alone establishes reuse or marketing rights.

**Data provenance** — Where a fact came from, when it was collected and under what access/use conditions. Essential for reviewing AI-extracted contact records.

**Deduplication** — Combining records that refer to the same real company, contact or vehicle while preserving their evidence and history.

**Refresh date and offer expiry** — A refresh date records when data was last checked; an offer expiry records how long stated commercial terms remain valid. They serve different purposes.
