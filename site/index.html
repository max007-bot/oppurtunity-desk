01  /  The problem

It is not a shortage of listings

Anyone can see the cars. What nobody can hold in their head at once is the rest of it.

Which car is still genuinely available, rather than still advertised. Which buyer said what, when, and whether they meant it. Whether a price is high or low against cars that are actually similar, rather than cars wearing the same badge. And whether the deal survives transport, VAT, registration tax and the money tied up while it happened.

Every one of those is knowable. They just live in four different places, and the answer goes stale at four different speeds.

02  /  The shape of it

Four things, and one rule

Observations

A car is a stream of sightings, not a row. Seen at this price, on this date, by this source. Nothing is ever overwritten, so you can always answer when it was last actually confirmed.

Reviewed facts

What a person has checked: the VIN, the mileage, the homologated seat count. When a source disagrees with a reviewed fact, the contradiction is shown and a human resolves it.

Requirements

What a named person at a named business said they wanted, on a date, and who confirmed it. Hard requirements are rejections, not deductions: four seats when five are required is a no.

Money

Comparable prices held apart by evidence type, costs entered as facts or left unknown, and a scenario that states what the deal contributes once everything is counted.

The rule: unknown is a real value, and it fails closed.

An unknown never becomes a zero, an average, or a convenient assumption. It blocks the thing that depends on it and names itself while doing so.

04  /  Design decisions

Seven choices that are the actual work

  1. Unknown blocks; it never becomes zero

    The commonest way a tool like this lies to you is by treating a missing cost as no cost. Here it returns Incomplete and names the fact it is waiting for.

  2. Money is never a floating-point number

    Integer minor units and an ISO currency, exact decimal arithmetic throughout. And 119.000 means a hundred and nineteen thousand in Prague and a hundred and nineteen in London, so a price without a locale is refused rather than guessed.

  3. Five kinds of price are never blended

    A supply asking price, a retail asking price, a dealer-to-dealer bid, a stated budget and a completed transaction describe five different things. Averaging them produces a number that describes no market at all.

  4. Absence is not a sale

    A failed check is recorded as a failed check. The last successful observation survives untouched, and the brief says the car may not be described as available. The embarrassing call is the one where you promise a car that went last week.

  5. Permission is a recorded human decision

    A discovered phone number is not consent. A contact policy names a person, a channel, a purpose and a date, and it expires. A suppression outranks every score in the system.

  6. Freshness has a clock, and the clock is injectable

    Supply confirmations go stale in 24 hours, comparables in 14 days, requirements in 30, contact details in 90. Nothing calls the system clock directly, which is why every stale badge is reproducible rather than dependent on when you looked.

  7. Demo and live are different databases

    Separate files, each stamped with its mode, every row flagged, and a guard that refuses to open one as the other. Demo data cannot leak into a real decision by accident.

05  /  Sources

What it is not connected to

Nothing. No live request has ever been made from this project.

The marketplaces sit in a source register as blocked — recorded, described, with no adapter behind them. Three other sources are implemented and tested offline, and all three are review required, so the fetch path refuses to run them.

The machinery for an authorised fetch is built and tested in full: it asks the policy first, opens a run record so a failure is accounted for rather than invisible, writes field-level evidence against every row it creates, and refuses outright if the approval has lapsed. What is missing is a signature, not code.

Not because the scraper would be hard.

Because the first question anyone sensible asks a supplier is whether they had permission, and the answer should be ready before the question.

06  /  Contact with reality

Four hours with real adverts

Thirty-eight public adverts from one exporter were read as research — not imported as stock, which would have made the demo look better and proved nothing. They changed three things.

What the adverts showedVerdictWhat changed
33 of 38 cars are new, at 0–30 km Reframed Not a used-car trader but a nearly-new export business, so the EU new-means-of-transport test is the mechanism of the trade, not a footnote. It is now a headline line on the vehicle record and on every call brief.
Listings are titled “S 450” and “S 580”, never “S-Class” Defect The matcher looked for the family name, so the largest cluster in the stock resolved to nothing. The tests missed it because the fixtures were built around the internal key, which does contain “s class”. The tests were checking a naming convention, not the world.
Seven S-Class cars span €131,000 to €192,000 Defect An S 450 and an S 580 share a generation, so both entered one comparable set. A median across that range describes no car anyone can buy. Variants are now held apart by model designation, so a suffix somebody did or did not type changes nothing.
13% of stock is a build slot, not a car Surfaced It was a badge among badges, which is easy to read past. The match screen now says it in a sentence, beside the date the buyer said they needed a car by.
GLE, GLC and GLS are a bigger cluster than the G 63 Added Model coverage of the observed stock moves from 61% to 82%. The long tail — one Rolls-Royce, one Ferrari, one ALPINA — still goes to review rather than being guessed at.

Four hours of contact with real adverts found defects that the specification, the test suite and the demo had all missed.

That is the argument for a pilot. It is also the argument against trusting any tool that has never met the data it claims to understand.

07  /  Limits

What it deliberately will not do

  • No live market coverage. It analyses imported observations and replayed snapshots, and it says so on every screen.
  • No outreach. No send, no dialler, no scheduler, no message credentials anywhere in the codebase.
  • No tax computation. It records reviewed assumptions with a source and a date. It does not calculate anyone's liability and does not pretend to.
  • No profit claim. It shows a conditional contribution and everything that contribution depends on.
  • No runtime model. Every figure on screen comes from a rule you can read. A confident-looking number with no traceable basis would be the opposite of the point.
  • No multi-user. One person, one machine, bound to localhost. Making it more than that is a rebuild, not a login screen, and the reasons are written down.

08  /  Next

What would make it real

In order of value. The first is worth more than the rest combined.

  1. An authorised stock export

    Any format. It turns replayed snapshots into genuine supply monitoring.

  2. Five to ten real buyer requirements

    The matching engine is the strongest part of the system and it is currently being fed entirely synthetic demand.

  3. Registration tax for one destination country

    With a source and a date. That is what turns Incomplete into a complete scenario.

  4. A decision on contact routes

    For one market, so the outreach gates have a real policy to enforce rather than a placeholder.

  5. Approval for one bounded live read

    The path is built, tested offline, and refuses to run until somebody records a review against the source.
